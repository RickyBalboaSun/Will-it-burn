    What's new

    • Spots searches by itself when you open it, for viewpoints within 25 km.
    • Miles and feet on US phones.
    • Tidier Today screen: forecast confidence sits under the score, and the week shows five days at a glance. Tap a day to plan ahead.
    • Community: see sunsets and sunrises shared from around the world on a map, and add your own photo after you rate a sky.
    • Will It Burn? is now Sunscore, with a new icon. Same forecasts, same 0–100 score.
    • Today tab: the main tab is now called Today, as it covers sunrises as well as sunsets.
    • One-tap rating at the bottom of Today: Dull, Some colour, Good, Great or On fire. Ratings are shared anonymously to improve the forecast.
    • Viewpoints near you in Spots: real viewpoints, beaches, cliffs and peaks, each scored, with a check that the view towards the sun is open and a Go button for directions.
    • Clear skies, scored for the air you're in: clean, dry air gives a clear sky far more colour than hazy, humid air, so a clear evening now scores from about 30 on a hazy coast to about 60 somewhere like the Utah desert.

    How it works

    The best sunsets happen when the sun, already at or below your horizon, shines underneath clouds above you and lights them from below.

    So the app checks the forecast in two places: overhead (is there cloud to light up?) and 75 km to 250 km towards the sun (is the sky clear enough for the light to get through?).

    • Cloud canvas: mid and high cloud overhead, ideally 30–70% cover.
    • Clear horizon: little low cloud along the light path.
    • No low cloud / not overcast: a thick deck overhead hides everything.
    • Dry and clear air: rain and haze wash the colours out.

    The six factors are multiplied together, so one bad factor pulls the whole score down. A clear sky scores about 25: a pleasant glow, but nothing to light up.

    The pictures are cartoons: the amount of cloud in each layer, the light path and the sun's position come from the forecast, but where each cloud sits in the picture is illustrative.

    Forecasts can be wrong, especially about cloud, so check the confidence and look again nearer the time. Your ratings help tune the model.
